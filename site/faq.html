Questions about credit scores

Plain answers to common questions. Answers about credit scores in Canada come from the Government of Canada: Financial Consumer Agency of Canada, Credit report and score basics.

What is a credit score?

A credit score is a 3-digit number that comes from your credit report. It shows how likely you are to repay money you borrow. In Canada, scores usually range from 300 to 900, and a higher score is better.

What affects my credit score?

The credit bureaus do not share their exact formulas. Common factors include missed payments, how much you owe, carrying a balance on credit cards, being close to or over your credit limit, how often you apply for new credit, and how long you have had credit.

Why can a card balance lower my score even if I pay on time?

How much you owe compared to your limit is one of the factors. For example, someone who regularly owes $4,500 on a card with a $5,000 limit may look riskier to lenders than someone who owes $1,000 each month.

Does paying rent on time count toward my credit score?

The Government of Canada's list of what a credit report may include names credit cards, loans, mortgages, and phone, internet and utility accounts. It does not list rent. thrifti credit is researching whether on-time rent and bills should count.

Does checking my own credit score lower it?

No. Checking your own credit report or score does not affect your credit score.

Does my credit history from another country count in Canada?

Canada's two main credit bureaus, Equifax and TransUnion, only collect information about your credit activity in Canada. Some lenders may look at your history from another country, but you may need to bring your own report from that country.

What is thrifti credit? Is it a lender?

thrifti credit is a research project on fairer credit scores in Canada. It is not a lender and does not offer loans today.

What is the CARE Score?

The CARE Score is a fairer way to look at credit that we are researching. It looks at five things: Consistent income, Affordable spending, Reliable payments (rent, utilities, phone on time), an Emergency cushion, and Staying in touch when money gets hard. It is still in development and is not used for any lending decision today.

What is the goal of the CARE Score?

To give a fairer picture of how people actually manage money. A regular credit score looks mostly at debt. The CARE Score also looks at steady income, everyday spending, rent and bills paid on time, and savings for emergencies. The goal is to help people who pay reliably but hit a hard stretch, so one bad year does not outweigh years of paying on time. The CARE Score is a research project. It is still in development and is not used for any lending decision today.

Will the CARE Score use credit history from other countries?

Yes. We plan for the CARE Score to use credit history from other countries, so newcomers do not have to start from zero. It is still in development and is not used for any lending decision today.
