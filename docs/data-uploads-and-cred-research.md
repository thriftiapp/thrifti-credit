# What customers can upload, and what CRED did

Research for AD, night of 12 to 13 November 2026. Every quoted rule was read on the regulator's or the company's own site tonight. This is not legal advice; a lawyer should confirm before launch.

## 1. What the rules say

**PIPEDA, Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada (OPC), Guidelines for obtaining meaningful consent**
- Financial information is "generally considered sensitive", so it needs **express consent** (a clear yes, not a pre-ticked box).
- We must explain, in plain words: what we collect, who receives it (the credit union), why, and the real risks.
- People "cannot be required to consent" to collection "beyond what is necessary to provide the product or service". Anything not essential must be a real, optional choice.
- Consent "is not a free pass" to collect data indiscriminately.

**OPC, Guidance on inappropriate data practices (PIPEDA s. 5(3), "no-go zones")**
- No profiling that "leads to unfair, unethical or discriminatory treatment contrary to human rights law".
- No collection for purposes "known or likely to cause significant harm".

**EU Directive 2023/2225, Article 18(3) (our chosen standard)**
- The assessment must use "relevant and accurate information".
- It "shall not include special categories of data" (health, religion, ethnicity and similar, under GDPR Article 9(1)).
- "Social networks shall not be considered as an external source."

## 2. Applied to each upload (my reading of the rules above)

| Upload | Allowed? | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| Credit card statements | Yes, with express consent | Directly relevant to card debt and the payoff loan. Statements list every purchase, so keep only balances, limits, rate, payments and late fees; do not store the merchant lines. |
| Bank statements | Yes, with express consent | Relevant to income, essentials and cash flow. Same care: purchases at pharmacies, clinics, places of worship or charities can reveal health or religion, which the EU standard bans from the rating. Keep categories and totals, drop merchant detail. |
| CRA notice of assessment | Yes, optional, uploaded by the customer | Verifies income. Already in the design. |
| Rent and utility bills | Yes, optional | Shows essentials paid on time. Core of the thrifti idea. |
| Grocery bills | Weak case, likely not | Hard to show it is necessary for a credit rating. Receipts can reveal diet, health or religion. Rewarding uploads with points could look like collecting more than needed. If ever used, only as a total grocery spend, never item lines, never required, and only after a lawyer agrees. |

Rules that apply to all of them: optional items stay optional (the app already shows "Optional"); points must never make an upload feel required; the customer can delete any upload; and the purpose is shown before the upload.

## 3. CRED (India): what is verified

- **Not bought by WhatsApp.** On 22 June 2026 CRED announced it will raise about US$900 million in a round **led by Meta as a minority investor**, at a valuation of about US$4.5 billion. Founder Kunal Shah steps down as CEO, keeps his shares and joins **Meta's global leadership team**; Miten Sampat is interim CEO. CRED's own release does not mention WhatsApp; press reports say Shah will lead WhatsApp, but CRED and Meta pages read tonight do not confirm that. (newsroom.cred.club)
- **Meta gets no member data**: no data-sharing agreement, no ad targeting, no AI training on CRED data. (CRED, "A note on trust")
- **Who can join: the opposite of thrifti.** CRED requires a credit score of 750 or above and an Indian mobile number. It is built for people who already have good credit. (cred.club FAQ)
- **How it hooks people:** credit card bill payments, payment reminders, hidden-fee checks and spending insights, rewarded with "cashback and coins". The home page does not mention uploading grocery or utility bills; I found no CRED source for bill uploads, so that part could not be confirmed.
- **Scale:** 17 million members engage monthly (June 2026); 1.26 crore monthly transacting users and revenue of ₹2,735 crore in FY2024-25; over half of new credit cards in India go to CRED members. Engagement drivers named: CRED Money, Credit Score and Card Management. No acquisition cost or growth-channel figures are published.

## 4. What thrifti can take from CRED
- Exclusivity and a "club" feel worked for them; thrifti can borrow the feel (Care for your tree, earned fruit) without the 750 gate, since our users are the people bureaus overlook.
- Rewarding a good habit (paying the bill) rather than an upload keeps rewards close to what matters for credit.
- A public "note on trust" with plain promises about data was central to their Meta deal. thrifti should publish one before launch.

## 5. How CRED makes money (from CRED's own FY2024-25 release)
- Revenue ₹2,735 crore, up 16%. Gross margin about 70%. Still loss-making: operating loss ₹298 crore, total loss ₹1,457 crore.
- Top three revenue drivers, in CRED's words: **lending, payments and insurance**. Its lending book reached ₹22,000 crore under management.
- CRED handles over a third of credit card bill payments in India.
- **Who pays is not published.** CRED's release does not say whether members, banks, brands or insurers pay, or how much each brings in. The split by segment is only in its company filings, which I did not read. So I cannot confirm that banks pay CRED for card use.
- What is clear from its own figures: CRED earns most where people borrow and pay with credit (lending, card bill payments), and over half of new credit cards in India go to its members. Its model grows with more credit use.

## 6. thrifti's version of the club: good habits, less card debt
Same feeling of belonging, opposite goal. Open to everyone, no score cutoff, and every reward points toward paying down cards, not using more.

| CRED idea | thrifti version |
|---|---|
| Members-only club, 750+ score | Open club. Your tree is your membership. Everyone starts as a seed. |
| Coins for paying card bills | Fruit for 12 months of on-time essentials. Points for effort (uploads). Perks "coming soon", never cash. |
| Bill reminders, hidden-fee checks | Reminders for rent, bills and loan payments. A card-interest meter: "this month your cards cost you $X". |
| Spending insights | "Card use" goal: help people get below 30% of their limits, shown on the tree. |
| Exclusive tiers | Tree stages: Seed, Sapling, Young tree, Full tree, earned by habits, never bought. |
| More credit products | One product: a lower-rate loan that pays cards off, plus rate step-downs for on-time payments. Celebrate a card paid off and closed. |

How thrifti makes money must match this: from the partner credit union for each loan that replaces costly card debt, never from more card use. That is to be agreed with the partner (not decided).
