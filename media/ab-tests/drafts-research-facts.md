## F1 (why people fall behind)
In Germany, about 187,000 people went to debt counselling in 2025. For more than half of them (54%), the main cause was job loss, illness, a separation or a business that failed. Poor budgeting was the main cause for only 12%.

Most people who fall behind are not careless. Something happened to them.

thrifti credit is a research project asking whether a credit rating should treat a hard event differently from a choice not to pay. If this has happened to you, your story would help: https://credit.thrifti.app/v2/?source=SRC

Source: Federal Statistical Office of Germany (Destatis), over-indebtedness statistics 2025.
Check: Destatis table "Ueberschuldung" 2025: unemployment 16.8%, separation/divorce/death of partner 11.1%, illness/addiction/accident 18.2%, failed self-employment 8.2% (sum 54.3%); poor budgeting 11.7%. Press release PD26_249: about 187,000 counselled persons.

## F2 (bills, not loans)
Across the EU in 2025, people on low incomes were more than 3 times as likely to be behind on utility bills as everyone else (16.8% compared with 5.1%). On loans the gap was much smaller (4.6% compared with 2.4%).

When money is tight, the bills for heat and light are often the first to slip. That says more about the cost of living than about whether someone repays their debts.

Source: Eurostat, EU statistics on income and living conditions (EU-SILC), 2025. "Low income" means below 60% of the median.
Check: Eurostat ilc_mdes07 and ilc_mdes08, EU27, 2025.

## F3 (what thrifti proposes, beyond the EU example)
New EU consumer credit rules, which apply from November 2026, give "more than 90 days" behind as an example of being in financial difficulty, and say lenders must offer reasonable forbearance before going to court.

In our research rating, CARE, we are testing a longer window: a bill or payment would only count against someone after 4 months unpaid, and the reason (job loss, illness, a separation) would be weighed. The goal is to help people recover, not push them further down.

Do you think 4 months is fair? Tell us in the comments.

Source: Directive (EU) 2023/2225, recital 81 and Article 35.
Note for AD: the EU 90 days is an example of "financial difficulty" in a recital, not a reporting rule. The post says it that way on purpose.
